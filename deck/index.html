EPUBs, ESP32s, and Everyone's a Developer Now07
where maintaining gets uncomfortable

Rejecting a PR from an experienced developer is just software development. Rejecting one from someone who bought a little e-reader, fell in love with your project, and learned GitHub over the weekend feels very different.

You're not just rejecting code. It can feel like you're rejecting their participation.

“maintaining standards” can quietly become a very efficient way to destroy the community that made you successful

EPUBs, ESP32s, and Everyone's a Developer Now08
and the stakes are physical

Bad firmware doesn't throw an exception.

It can leave somebody holding a tiny plastic rectangle that no longer turns on. There's no app store between us and the device in their hand.

Once hundreds of thousands of people trust your software, stability is a feature too.

what I got wrong about this job

The scarce resource isn't code.
We have more code than we know what to do with.

Judgment.Trust.Responsibility.

Someone has to decide what belongs in the core. Someone has to protect the boring battery fix from the exciting rewrite. Someone has to make sure the firmware found through a six-month-old TikTok video won't brick a brand-new device. And someone has to build the places where ideas can flourish when the answer for core is no.
